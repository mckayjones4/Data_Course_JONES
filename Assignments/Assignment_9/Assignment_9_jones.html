I am going to model on admit, and define 4 different potential models.

mod1 = glm(formula = as.logical(admit) ~(gpa+gre)*rank,
           data = grad_dat,
           family = 'binomial')

grad_dat$pred1 <- predict(mod1, grad_dat, type = 'response')

grad_dat %>%
  ggplot(aes(x = gpa,
             y = pred1,
             color = factor(rank))) +

Model 4 has the lowest AIC scores, but all of the models are relatively similar with their RMSE and R2 scores. Looking at the plots, the models are performing very similarly. I’m going to do some cross validation to compare how model 2 and model 4 perform.

I’m splitting the data into training and testing sets.

id <- createDataPartition(grad_dat$admit, p = 0.8, list = FALSE)

train <- grad_dat[id,]
test <- grad_dat[-id,]

I’m now going to retrain the models.

mod2_formula <- mod2$formula
mod2 <- glm(data = train, formula = mod2_formula)

mod4_formula <- mod4$formula
mod4 <- glm(data = train, formula = mod4_formula)

Takeaways

-Both models have low R2 (variance) values, but Model 4 explains slightly more variance.

-Model 4 has a slightly lower RMSE value (which is better).

-Model 4 has a slightly lower Sigma value.

-Model 4 has higher AIC/AICc weights.

-Model 2 has higher BIC weights, which suggests that Model 2 is simpler and preferred under BIC.

-The overall performance scores strongly favor Model 4 (83.33%) over Model 2 (16.67%).

-Model 4 is the better model overall.
